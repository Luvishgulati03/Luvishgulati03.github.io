the problem.

I did not want a chat window. I wanted the thing a good chief of staff does: hold the context I keep dropping, notice the work I keep postponing, and come back to me with something already drafted.

Every assistant I tried failed in the same two places. It forgot, so every session started from zero and I paid the setup cost again. And it was too eager, happy to send, post or apply on my behalf in a way I would not trust with my own name attached.

So the constraints were set before the first line: it has to remember across sessions, it has to run on the laptop I already own, it has to cost nothing per run, and anything that can reach the outside world has to be on a leash I hold.

the architecture.

Henry is a spine, not a pile of scripts. Inputs converge on one routing brain, the brain decides which capability and which model tier the request deserves, and a two-store memory sits alongside the whole thing, feeding recall in and taking writes back out.

That shape is what made Bose possible: the same spine, redeployed into a school with different surfaces and a different approval model.

The spine. One routing brain in front of everything, so adding a capability is adding a route rather than another entry point. Memory sits beside the spine rather than inside a module, which is why recall works across capabilities instead of within one.

the rails.

The most interesting engineering in Henry is the part that stops it doing things. An agent with my accounts attached is only as good as the limits around it, so the limits are structural rather than prompt-level.

  • One post a day, hardThe daily tech-tweet pipeline cannot post twice. It is not asked nicely to behave, it is capped, and there is a kill switch that stops the pipeline outright.
  • Every post mirrored to meAnything published in public is also sent to me. If Henry says something in my name, I find out from Henry first, not from someone replying to it.
  • A single update pumpAll Telegram traffic runs through one loop that owns polling. Two consumers racing for the same updates is the classic way a bot double-sends, so there is only ever one.
  • Eval-gated retrievalRanking changes to the knowledge RAG ship only when the eval harness agrees they are an improvement. Retrieval quality is the kind of thing that degrades invisibly, so it gets a gate rather than a vibe check.
  • Format-locked outputResume and cover-letter tailoring has to return the exact document format, not free prose. A model that drifts into helpful commentary produces something I cannot actually send.

Why this matters more than the feature list: the rails are the reason the same architecture could be handed to a school. Swap "I approve" for "the teacher approves" and the guardrail layer is already there. That is the whole extraction story behind Bose.

what's live.

All of this is shipped and running as a daily driver, in TypeScript ESM under a deliberate zero-heavy-dependencies doctrine, on an 8 GB M1 Air.
